- The planner's cost model is fitted to measurements. For an aggregation over a table of a million rows or more, the pushed Lance scan and the `LuceneAggregateExec` alternative are each priced in predicted milliseconds by `CostModel`: a fixed cost per request, an object store open latency for `s3://`, `gs://`, `az://` and similar table URIs, the per row work of every thread (rows divided by the fan out node count and by `lance.aggregation.pushdown_parallelism` or `lance.fragment_path.slices`) with one coefficient per kind of group key and metric, the object store transfer of the columns the scan reads, a hash table penalty above a million groups, and the merge of the parallel scans' group rows. The coefficients (`CostCoefficients`) were fitted by non negative least squares to the warm latencies of the aggregation shapes measured on the 20M, 100M and 1B row tables across 1 to 6 node clusters with the pushdown on and off (`src/test/resources/cost/measurements.csv`, `scripts/fit-cost-coefficients.py`, `src/test/resources/cost/fit-report.md`), and `CostModelTests` holds the model to the measured choices. Callers hand the planner a `CostInputs` (fan out nodes, storage kind from the table URI, CPUs, the two settings) through `LancePlannerFactory.plan(RelNode, CostInputs)`; `GET /{index}/_lance/explain` builds them from the cluster state, so a plan over a large table now depends on the table size, the node count and the storage kind: a keyword `terms` or a metric only aggregation over a billion rows on S3 plans as `LuceneAggregateExec` where the pushed scan used to win unconditionally. Tables below a million rows and every hits shape keep the previous ordering. (#155)
